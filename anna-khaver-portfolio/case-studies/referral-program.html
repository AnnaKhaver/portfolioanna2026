The old program

Manual for the team, silent for users

No updates for users

Task

What it needed

A program people trust, and a tool to run it

For customers

  • Share by link, social channels or an email invite, after accepting the terms and conditions
  • A Referral Status page with filters
  • Claim the reward directly from the page, with Amazon and PayPal options

For the team

  • A reward review in the Backoffice with fraud flags, related referrals, an audit log and a CSV export for finance
  • Manual enrol and unenrol
  • Multiple programs, each with its own threshold, reward types and currencies

Built in

  • Automatic reward expiry
  • Multi-region support
  • Privacy: data is wiped when a referrer churns, and sensitive fraud data is cleaned up after a retention period

Designed with legal and support: fraud-state messaging and consent to the terms and conditions. Today one program runs, the gift card Tell-a-Friend, and the foundation is ready for more.

Action

Mid stateFirst design ideas

A first idea: status, analytics and claiming on one page

An early iteration on the way to the final design. It brought the referral journey onto a single page, so a person could share, follow each invite and claim a reward without leaving it.

ReviewDesign review session

Feedback from colleagues shaped the final design

After the mid-state design, the draft went to a design review session with colleagues. Sticky notes on the board carried their comments, and several of them changed how the page is built.

Clearer hierarchy

The page looked overwhelming. People asked for a visual hierarchy that shows what matters most, and for the sharing entry point to sit close to the top.

Separate the sections

One long page was a lot. Several people suggested tabs, and keeping the analytics and the status list close together, since they are connected topics.

Lighter status labels

Too many gray text pills made the table hard to scan and look disabled. Ideas included icons for how someone joined and check marks for created accounts.

Status without the internal process

Showing status helps, but users may not need to know about internal steps such as the manual fraud check. Breaking it into steps keeps people from being left without information.

What changed: the final design splits the page into two tabs, How it works and Referral status, with sharing at the top.

FinalThe new referral page

Two tabs: how it works, and where every referral stands

The invite field and sharing options stay at the top. Below them, the page adapts to where the person is: a short explainer before the first invite, and a status table once referrals are in motion.

DetailsEmpty states

Empty states that point to the next step

The design goes into the details. An empty state never leaves a dead end: it says why there is nothing to show and offers the next action.

AddedFeedback in the flow

Feedback at any time

We are feedback-driven and data-driven, so feedback became part of the flow. At the bottom of the status page a quick rating asks how easy the program was to use, and a link is always there for anyone who wants to say more.

How it works

A referral, from invite to gift card

Meet Uko, who refers, and Magda, who signs up. Every step has a clear status and an email, so nobody has to ask where things stand.

  1. Uko is enrolled

    He becomes a paying Pipedrive customer and is quietly enrolled in the program. He gets his own promo code, built into his referral link.

    Email: enrolment

  2. Uko shares the link

    On the referral page he accepts the terms and conditions, then shares by copy link, email invite, LinkedIn, X or Facebook.

  3. Magda signs up

    She signs up with Uko’s promo code and gets a 30-day trial instead of the usual 14. The referral shows in his Referral Status tab, and a few background checks run.

    Email: referral signed up

  4. Magda starts paying

    Every payment counts. Once her payments reach the program’s threshold, in whatever currency she pays, the referral is qualified and a reward is created for Uko, waiting for review.

    Email: referral qualified

  5. The review

    The reward lands in the Backoffice queue. A reviewer checks fraud signals, Uko’s other referrals and the audit log, then approves it.

    Email: reward approved, with the claim deadline

  6. Uko claims his reward

    A Claim button appears on his referral page. He picks Amazon or PayPal, enters his details and submits.

    Email: reward claimed

  7. The gift card is issued

    Backoffice admins send Amazon gift cards. The finance team sends PayPal rewards from the CSV report. The reward is marked as issued.

    Email: reward issued

Every state has a label

When things don’t go to plan

Referral
Invited→Signed up→Paying→Qualified·Inactive
Reward
In review→Approved→Claimed→Issued·InvalidatedExpired

Canceled

Magda cancels before her payments reach the threshold. The referral becomes inactive, no reward is ever created, and the page shows a “canceled” pill.

Ineligible

She cancels after the referral qualified, while the reward waits for review or approval. The reward is invalidated, the page shows an “ineligible” pill, and no email is sent.

Expired

An approved reward that isn’t claimed within a year expires on its own, shows an “Expired” pill and can no longer be edited in the Backoffice.

Under the hood: the program is event-driven, so enrolment, signups, qualification and reward changes show up in near real time, and it runs across regions.

Feedback from the Backoffice admin

Four ways it improved the review workflow

From spreadsheet to plugin

Each claim arrives as its own entry with direct links to the relevant accounts, so there is no more hunting for what is needed.

Automatic checks and rejections

The plugin checks key details on each claim against the set rules. Claims that don’t meet them are rejected automatically, which removes a lot of manual review.

Better visibility for users

Users can follow every step of the validation and get email notifications, which has reduced support cases about claim status.

Time savings

Validating 10 claims used to take around 30 minutes. The same batch now takes around 10.

“Overall, the changes have made the process faster, less manual, and less interruptive for me, and the experience is clearer for users too.”
