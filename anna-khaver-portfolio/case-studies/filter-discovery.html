Situation

How we found the problem

Few new users discover filters

≈8%

of trialists use filters, and the share keeps declining

≈42%

of new paying customers use filters, a stable share

The problem surfaced in an ideation workshop at the end of January. Notes on pinned and quick filters, empty states and sample data pointed to one gap: users accept default views and never discover filters. Amplitude data confirmed low trial usage.

Source: Amplitude and the January 2026 ideation workshop board

What deeper research told us

Filtering is hard to start

35%

of filter support contacts are basic questions, one of the top reasons users consider churning (power user research, 2023)

Finding filters is hard, and creating or deciding which filter is needed for your case takes a lot of time.

Earlier research showed that segmenting data with filters is complex and discoverability is low. Light users rarely filter, while power users rely on filters heavily. The experiment targeted users who never started.

Source: Quick filters learnings presentation and filters power user research

Task

What we set out to do

Get new users to try filters when it makes the most sense for them

The first idea, pre-created quick filters based on user needs, was reshaped into a suggested filter banner and tested as an A/B experiment.

GoalRaise awareness and first-time use of filters
Primary metricTrial-to-paid conversion (T2P)
Supporting metricShare of trial users who use quick filters
AudienceEnglish-speaking new users who never filtered
WhereContact and organisation list views

The goal was to lower the effort of starting to filter. T2P is the primary metric; quick filter usage shows whether the banner changes behavior.

Source: Filter discovery experiment · Airtable

Action

What we did

Aligned, designed, built and tested

Align

  • Sized the audience: 10+ entities reached at most 18% of trials
  • Aligned defaults with the parallel data-organisation test
  • Picked a fixed set of existing users who never filtered

Design and build

  • Designed a quick intro banner that suggests a reasonable filter with an Apply filter button
  • Added a coach mark on the Save button for future use
  • Set up tracking

Test and launch

  • Ran two async bug bash sessions in April
  • Shipped final fixes and took the experiment live
  • Launched the A/B test in Amplitude the same day

Three phases: agree scope with product, analytics and the neighbouring experiment; design and build the banner; bug bash and launch on 16 April 2026.

Choosing the filters

Deciding which filters to show

A suggested filter only works if it is one people actually use. We looked at which filters new customers reach for most, so the banner would suggest something relevant.

Alternative ideas

Exploring other ways to introduce filters

Before settling on the banner, we explored several directions on one board: a banner with steps, a banner with arrows that show more possible filters, a version with a call to action, and a sandbox for trying filters, either as a separate space or with the banner included.

Rollout and learnings

Rolled out, with lessons for the next test

Rollout

  • From 12 June 2026, visible to admin company creators with at least one contact or organization
  • Covers all new companies and the previous treatment group
  • Live behind a feature flag, and the Amplitude experiment is archived

What we learned

  • Suggesting a relevant filter lowers the effort to start: 7.1% of treatment users applied one from the banner
  • The uplift grew to 13% by June, and we kept reporting the conservative 8.4%
  • ARR is scaled by exposure: only 24.4% of trial users open the list views with the banner

Source: Slack #filter-discovery-experiment, Jun 2026
